# Hypothesis register: multi-card proof program

As of 2026-08-19. Format per the workbench register: research disposition first (CONFIRMED | REFINED | CONTRADICTED | CONDITIONALLY APPROVED | position | pending), then `RESOLVED (date, author)` recording Murali's adjudication. Parent rows: workbench H09 (mechanism distinctive, REFINED) and H12 (enterprise transfer, CONDITIONALLY APPROVED, unproven empirically). This program exists to resolve them with measurements.

| ID | Hypothesis (author's position) | Tested by | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Aspect dilution is real and material | S0 corrected with inferred cards and a matched chunk control; E1a LIMIT; E1b SciFact (Sprint R1, done) | **CONFIRMED for the pooling comparison, and now conditional for the card comparison.** Pooled retrieval collapses as aspects accumulate, from 0.815 nDCG@10 at k=1 to 0.294 at k=10, so multi-vector representation is unambiguously better than a single vector on aspect-rich material. Against a matched chunk control the picture is two-sided: inferred cards beat chunks from k=5 upward while using fewer embeddings (+0.109 at k=10 with 9.6 units per document against 12.8, p=0.0001, Holm-corrected), and lose on SciFact where queries concern whole documents (-0.032, p=0.012). Caveat resolved 2026-08-20: hand-written anchors, carrying none of the generator's phrasing, roughly double the effect rather than shrinking it (+0.188 at k=10, p=0.0001, at 25 percent fewer embeddings than the chunk control), because a topical description is a better aspect prototype than one rendered sentence. The oracle taxonomy was a handicap, not an advantage. |
| P2 | The win is purpose framing, not just chunking | E1a LIMIT; E1b SciFact at matched capacity (Sprint R1, done) | **CONTRADICTED on real prose.** At matched units per document (2.8 cards against 2.7 chunks) multi-card scores 0.635 nDCG@10 against chunking's 0.667: -0.032, 95% CI [-0.057, -0.008], p=0.0115, 70 losses to 36 wins. Equalising the budget barely moved the gap, so capacity was not the explanation and the cards are genuinely worse here. The LIMIT result that appeared to support this row does not survive its own control either: a blind three-word window recovers 0.610 of the 0.674 attributed to alignment, and bare attribute strings beat the framed cards. Refined claim: multi-vector beats pooling, the benefit tracks embedding count, and aspect framing helps only where the taxonomy matches the query distribution and hurts where it does not. |
| P3 | Gate separability transfers to enterprise-flavored corpora: composite-cosine gate fit on public data achieves useful AUC (target: ROC-AUC at or above 0.85 on held-out labeled Enron, with a defensible threshold at recall 0.95) | E2a gate study, Enron against Usenet with exact provenance labels (Sprint R1, done); within-corpus version needs the human-audited sample (G1) | **SUPPORTED on the easy version, with two qualifications that matter more than the headline.** Held-out ROC-AUC 0.933 and PR-AUC 0.935, above the 0.85 target. Qualification one: fitting the anchor weights is worth almost nothing, 0.933 against 0.931 for equal weights, so the discrimination comes from having several purpose-specific anchor sets rather than from how they are mixed. Qualification two: at recall 0.95 the gate discards only 33.7 percent of the corpus, and at recall 0.99 only 12.1 percent, so the filter contributes a linear factor of one to two rather than the order of magnitude the framing implies. Separating email from Usenet is easier than separating on-purpose material within one corpus, so this is an upper bound on discrimination. |
| P4 | The economics claim is a measurable scaling law: two-pass cost per 1k documents is at least an order of magnitude below full-LLM per-document processing, and the fitted cost-vs-N slopes diverge | E2b on 20,000 Enron messages with topic coverage reported (Sprint R1, done) | **PARTLY CONTRADICTED. The scaling law does not survive; a large constant-factor saving does.** With topic coverage held to useful granularity, topics grow superlinearly (N^1.245) and the cost advantage *shrinks* with corpus size (N^-0.273), the opposite of the claim. The apparent widening at the original setting came from clusters coarsening as N grew: 14 topics over 10,000 messages at 485 documents per topic is not a usable summary. What remains defensible is a constant-factor saving of roughly 200x at comparable granularity, itself flattered by pricing encoder compute at zero and by comparing designs that produce different deliverables. See reviews/r1-red-team-2026-08-20.md. |
| P5 | Consumer-dependent diversity flip (the kernel of the provisional) | E3a retrieval side; E3b consumer study, three judges including one cross-family (Sprint R1, done) | **RETRIEVAL HALF SUPPORTED. CONSUMER HALF NOT SUPPORTED.** The harvest sits on the efficient frontier, buying coverage about twice as cheaply as MMR and admitting fewer answerless documents than MMR or a DPP; that half stands. The consumer interaction does not: it measures +0.071 (p=0.009) under gemini-2.5-flash, +0.047 (p=0.211) under gemini-2.5-pro, and +0.008 (p=0.763) under Azure GPT-5.4. The effect shrinks monotonically with distance from the generator's own family and vanishes cross-family, which is the signature of a judging artefact. Not disproved, since all three point estimates are positive and 60 tasks cannot resolve an effect this small, but no evidence may be cited for it. Robust and judge-independent: harvesting reduces unsupported content in the output (human -0.067, p=0.0018), which was not predicted and is the study's most defensible finding. |
| P6 | Identifier and exact-match queries need the lexical hybrid: dense-only and multicard-only degrade on identifier query classes; RRF fusion recovers them | E1a LIMIT (Sprint R1, partial); E1 query class B slice on C1 (Sprint R2) | **SUPPORTED IN PART.** On LIMIT, whose queries name a specific attribute and so behave like exact-match lookups, BM25 reaches 0.997 nDCG@10 against 0.314 for pooled dense, and the hybrid matches BM25 at 0.997 while dense-multicard alone reaches 0.988. The lexical component is doing real work and costs nothing when fused. Enron identifier queries remain the proper test. |

| H-DYN-2 | LLM-designed taxonomy (corpus sample + train workload) beats the hand-written taxonomy and at least ties matched chunks on SciFact | e1_llm_taxonomy, sealed before the prereg, unsealed on approval 2026-08-20 | **NOT SUPPORTED.** The designer produced a sensible content taxonomy (molecular mechanisms, disease pathology, experimental methods, physiological systems, clinical outcomes) that tied the hand-written taxonomy (-0.009, p=0.48) and remained significantly below matched chunks (-0.042, 95% CI [-0.069,-0.016], p=0.0018). Interpretation per the frozen rules: on workloads whose queries concern whole findings rather than aspects, no view taxonomy helps, LLM-designed or not. SciFact marks the method's domain boundary; the live claims now rest on H-DYN-1, 3, 4 and 6. |

| H-DYN-1 | Designed view sets beat an instruction-conditioned single embedding given the same objective, at matched spend | e_dyn hdyn1, synthetic workload A and SciFact | **NOMINALLY SUPPORTED, BUT THE BASELINE IS NOT SOUND AND THE VERDICT SHOULD NOT BE USED.** Designed views beat the instruction arm on both corpora (synthetic +0.310, SciFact +0.244, both p=0.0001). The problem is the arm itself: e5-small-v2 is not an instruction-following retriever, it uses fixed query and passage prefixes, and prepending the objective text to each query degraded it below the plain pooled baseline (SciFact 0.380 against 0.550, -0.170, p=0.0001). The comparison therefore measures query dilution, not instruction conditioning. The frozen fallback rule permitted this encoder after instructor-base failed to load, so the run is protocol-compliant, but the hypothesis is unresolved and must be rerun against a genuine instruction-following retriever (Promptriever, InstructOR, GSTransform) on hardware that can load them. |
| H-DYN-3 (round 2) | Same ladder, inside an objective-gated pool with guided topics and LLM noise cleaning | e_dyn2 ladder | **NOT SUPPORTED, and unstable across operating points.** The objective increment is +0.019 (p=0.25) at gate keep 0.35, -0.061 (p=0.0018) at 0.50, and -0.012 (p=0.56) at 0.75: it changes sign and is significant in the harmful direction at one setting, so no stable effect exists either way and the single-workload comparison must not be quoted without its sweep. Earlier reading, retained: **the penalty is gone** relative to round 1's -0.068. L0 pooled 0.261, L1 chunks 0.483, L4 corpus-only 0.540, L5 objective 0.530; the objective increment is -0.010 (p=0.4587) against round 1's significant -0.068, and stays non-significant across the gate sweep. Restoring the gate removed the harm that round 1 measured, which confirms the diagnosis that narrow objective views were being polluted by off-objective content, but objective conditioning still does not beat a well-formed corpus-only taxonomy on a single workload. |
| H-DYN-3 (round 1, no gate) | Objective conditioning adds more than corpus conditioning (L5 > L4, and the objective increment exceeds the corpus increment) | e_dyn hdyn3, conditioning ladder on synthetic workload A | **CONTRADICTED, in the opposite direction.** The ladder reads L0 pooled 0.295, L2 generic 0.449, L1 chunks 0.588, L5 corpus-plus-objective 0.605, L4 corpus-only 0.673. Corpus conditioning is worth a great deal (+0.224 over generic views, p=0.0001) and beats blind chunks. Adding the research objective made things significantly worse (-0.068, p=0.0020). On this evidence the useful signal is the corpus, which is what CAMI-class and TnT-LLM-class systems already condition on, and the objective actively narrowed the view set in a way that cost recall. |
| H-DYN-4 (round 2, gate plus guided topics plus LLM cleaning) | Same cross-over, with the three missing pipeline stages restored and both taxonomies scored over a common per-workload gated pool | e_dyn2 crossover, gate-strength sweep at keep 0.35, 0.50, 0.75 | **SUPPORTED AT TWO OF THREE OPERATING POINTS after a declared repair; see the pre- and post-repair tables in sprints/sprint-r2-dynamic.md.** Post-repair: keep 0.35 A +0.070 (p=0.0001) B +0.066 (p=0.0029) SUPPORTED; keep 0.50 A +0.114 (p=0.0001) B +0.035 (p=0.0153) SUPPORTED; keep 0.75 A +0.156 (p=0.0001) B +0.006 (p=0.59) NOT SUPPORTED. The A direction is robust everywhere and grows as the gate loosens; the B direction holds at the two tighter gates. Earlier reading, retained: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, one direction robust and one fragile.** The A direction holds at every operating point (+0.067, +0.062, +0.156, all p=0.0001); the B direction reaches significance only at keep 0.50 (+0.062, p=0.0001) against +0.023 (p=0.15) and +0.006 (p=0.59) elsewhere. The preregistered rule requires both directions, so the hypothesis is met at one setting of three and the full sweep is reported rather than the favourable column. The asymmetry traces to a diagnosed defect: the B taxonomy was built on a degenerate 3-topic model against A's 12, despite equal seeding. Two confounds were removed before believing any of it: per-taxonomy gated pools (which halved the effect when corrected to a common pool) and a writer-schema crash. |
| H-DYN-4 (round 1, no gate, unguided topics) | Cross-over: a taxonomy designed for objective A beats one designed for B on A's workload, and vice versa | e_dyn hdyn4, two objectives over one corpus | **NOT SUPPORTED. No cross-over of any size.** T_A on workload A 0.605 against T_B's 0.585 (+0.020, p=0.35); T_B on workload B 0.782 against T_A's 0.784 (-0.002, p=0.92). Taxonomies designed under different objectives serve each other's workloads indistinguishably. This was the decisive test of whether the objective does the work, and the answer is that it does not. |
| H-DYN-6 | Topic-stratified sampling beats random sampling on minority-topic queries of a skewed corpus, with no gap on a balanced corpus | e_dyn hdyn6, BERTopic sampler, skewed and balanced corpora | **NOT SUPPORTED, and the negative control fired.** The mechanism works exactly as designed: on the skewed corpus the stratified sample covered 100 percent of minority topics against 60 percent for random and relevance sampling, with BERTopic finding 25 topics at 4.8 percent noise. But it bought nothing: minority-query retrieval was -0.015 (p=0.70) against random. Worse for the hypothesis, on the BALANCED corpus, where the preregistered control said the gap should vanish, stratified sampling won significantly (+0.052 all queries p=0.0009, +0.058 minority p=0.029). An effect that appears where it should not and is absent where it should be is not the claimed mechanism. |

| H-DYN-7 | Conditioning the view designer on business context and schema metadata beats no context, on a varied line-of-business workload | e_dyn3 round 4, preregistered before implementation | **NOT SUPPORTED, and harmful.** Sample-only design scored 0.562 nDCG@10; adding business context dropped it to 0.515 (-0.047, p=0.0001), adding schema metadata to 0.494 (-0.068 against sample-only, p=0.0001), and adding a narrow objective to 0.457 (-0.037 against metadata, p=0.0179). The harm is monotone in the amount of context supplied. The preregistered convergence condition did not fire (mean pairwise taxonomy cosine 0.833), so the taxonomies were genuinely different and genuinely worse: context produced four elegant business-shaped views where the corpus itself yielded six that covered it better. **This triggers the preregistered stopping rule and the programme ceases pursuing the conditioning claim.** |
| H-SEL-1 (round 5, synthetic) | Moving the objective LATE (selection from a shared, unseeded substrate) reproduces the cross-over on the validated card builder | e_dyn4 synthetic, 5 topic-model seeds | **NOT SUPPORTED.** Two-sided cross-over at 1 of 5 seeds. A direction robustly positive (+0.034 to +0.099, significant in 4/5); B direction near zero or wrong sign. The cross-over lived in the objective SEEDS of rounds 2 to 3, not in late selection; the practical, embed-once version does not carry it. |
| H-SEL-2 (round 5, real data) | Objective-selected cards beat a corpus-only card set and an instruction-conditioned encoder on human-judged data, and follow the instruction | e_dyn4 FollowIR Core17 (20 q, 3 seeds; validated-representation confirmation seed 13) and News21 (32 q, 2 seeds) | **NOT SUPPORTED, and objective conditioning is harmful.** Core17: objective-selected cards are the worst arm (og 0.375 mean-pool; 0.349 validated concat) against corpus-only cards (0.400; 0.409), a penalty of about 0.06 for conditioning, representation-independent. News21: the instruction-prepended encoder is the best arm (og 0.416) and every card arm is worse. p-MRR about zero for both instruction-aware arms on both collections: card re-selection does not follow the instruction. Durable positive: corpus-only multi-view indexing stays competitive with chunks and pooling. |
| H-DYN-8 | Zero-shot topic modelling preserves discovered structure better than seeded, without losing retrieval | e_dyn3 round 4 | **INCONCLUSIVE, diagnostic invalid.** All three modes returned identical topic counts and noise fractions, and the preservation measure was computed as topic count minus supplied count, which is arithmetic rather than a test of whether discovered topics survived. Testing this properly requires content-level matching of discovered topics against supplied ones. No conclusion drawn. |

## Rules

- A disposition is written only by the research agent citing the experiment artifacts; RESOLVED is written only by Murali.
- Pre-agreed honest fallback for P1/P2 (from the approved plan): if chunk-max ties multicard, the public claim becomes "multi-embedding beats pooling; purpose framing adds X" with the measured X, and P2 is marked accordingly.
- P5 results below the judge-agreement floor are stamped NOT_CALIBRATED and are barred from the paper (eval plane 1).
- Nothing in this file is published as-is; public drafts are sanitized derivations (workbench convention).
